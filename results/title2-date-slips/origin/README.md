## Headline

**All three predictions held.** The wrong day was in print before the rule was. The Justice
Department's interim final rule was signed April 16, 2026, put on public inspection at the Federal
Register on April 17 (as vendor and law-firm summaries report; the public-inspection copy itself was
not read) and published April 20. Pages dated April 17 to 19 already gave the new dates, and
2 of the 5 that give an exact 2027 day give April 24, 2027 (Delaware's Government Information Center on
April 17 and Community College Daily on April 19). After publication it was 1 of 9 (the National
Association of Counties, April 20). The Justice Department's own pages give April 26, 2027.

**A pattern found while reading (not pre-registered):** all 3 wrong pages also give the 2024 rule's
*second* date as April 24, 2027, when the 2024 rule said April 26, 2027. All 9 right pages that mention
that second date give April 26, 2027. So the wrong day seems to start from a wrong idea of the old
rule: if both old dates are taken to be April 24 (2026 and 2027), adding "a full year" gives April 24,
2027 and April 24, 2028. NACo's page already gave the old small-county date as "April 24, 2027" in its
update of September 22, 2025, seven months before the extension.

## Why the 26th

The 2024 rule was published Wednesday, April 24, 2024. Its first date, April 24, 2026, fell on a
Friday. Three years after publication would be Saturday, April 24, 2027, and the rule's second date
was Monday, April 26, 2027. The 2026 rule moved the first date to that same day, April 26, 2027, and the
second to April 26, 2028 (28 CFR 35.200(b), read on eCFR on 2026-11-14). Adding one year to April 24,
2026 gives a Saturday that is not in either rule.

## The Justice Department's pages (O3)

The ADA.gov fact sheet on the web rule gives April 26, 2027 and April 26, 2028, and the reader found no
"April 24, 2027" on it. eCFR's 28 CFR 35.200(b) gives "Beginning April 26, 2027" and "Beginning April 26,
2028". A search of ada.gov, justice.gov and federalregister.gov for "April 24, 2027" with Title II
returned no page giving that day. The rule's PDF on ADA.gov could not be read with the tools used
(compressed streams), and no Justice Department press release on the extension was found on
justice.gov.

## Limits

Eight searches on one day; results change by time, place and person, and five of the eight queries
contain one of the two days being compared (three April 24, 2027, two April 26, 2027). 14 dated pages with an exact day is small: O2's difference is a pattern, not a
rate. The second-date pattern was noticed after reading and was not predicted. Pages were read through
an AI web reader that quotes the page; the two pre-publication wrong pages were each read twice. A page
may show new text under an old date. This reads what pages say; the wrong day is two days early, so
nobody who plans to it is late, but statements that copy it give a date the rule does not have.
