# Title II date slips, part 12: does an old wrong date predict the new wrong date? (2026-11-15, pre-registered)

Made by MV2 of Munim, Inc. with AI agents; not yet checked by a person. Text and data CC BY 4.0.
Plan: [PREREG.md](PREREG.md), registered 2026-11-15 at about 07:40 UTC before any search for this part.
Every page read: [pages.csv](pages.csv).

## Headline

**The idea could not be tested this way, and what was found leans against it.** Part 11 noticed,
without predicting it, that the 3 pages giving the wrong new first day "April 24, 2027" also gave
the 2024 rule's second date as April 24, 2027 (the rule said April 26, 2027). This part looked for
publishers that wrote the old second date before the April 2026 extension and checked what they
wrote afterwards.

- Six fixed searches found only **2 publishers whose pages dated before April 17, 2026 give only the
  wrong old date** (CITI Program, February 2026; Spedtacular Daily Living, January 2026), and 2 more
  that give both days (Accessible.org on two different pages; ADA Scanner in one page's body and its
  FAQ). The plan needed at least 5 (R1 failed). None is a government body.
- **For 3 of those 4, no new date was found at all.** Site searches of CITI Program, Spedtacular
  Daily Living and ADA Scanner return only pages that give the 2024 dates as current, read on
  2026-11-15, seven months after the extension.
  So there was almost nothing to follow up: R2, R3 and R4 could not be graded.
- **Outside the count, the pages that state both an old and a new date go the other way.** Two pages
  give the old second date as April 24, 2027 and still give the new first day right (GovBuilt,
  undated; City of Anywhere, June 30, 2026). Level Access (June 2, 2026) gives the old second date
  right, as April 26, 2027, and the new first day wrong, as April 24, 2027. Accessible.org had the
  old date wrong on a 2024 page and gave the new first day right on April 17, 2026. So part 11's
  3-of-3 pattern is not a rule: among vendors, the wrong new day comes with a right old date too, and
  a wrong old date often comes with a right new day.

## The counted pages (dated before April 17, 2026)

Group E, old second date April 24, 2027:

- CITI Program, posted February 25, 2026: "Public entities serving a population of fewer than 50,000
  must comply by April 24, 2027." No extension mentioned; two follow-up searches of its site found no
  new date.
- Spedtacular Daily Living, January 8, 2026: "April 24, 2027: public entities serving fewer than
  50,000, and special district governments of that size." Its August 24, 2026 article still gives the
  2024 dates as current, this time with April 26, 2027.
- Accessible.org, schools guide, May 7, 2024: "and April 24, 2027 for smaller entities and special
  district governments" (its fact sheet, updated March 10, 2025, gives April 26, 2027).
- ADA Scanner, March 20, 2026: body "April 26, 2027", FAQ "April 24, 2027 for smaller entities". No
  new date found on its site.

Group C, old second date April 26, 2027: the New York State Bar Association (April 2, 2026; the same
page, updated April 21, gives April 26, 2027 and April 26, 2028), Aberdeen Broadcast Services
(April 7, 2026; no new date found) and Accessible.org's fact sheet.

## A possible federal source of the old wrong date (not counted)

The SBA Office of Advocacy's post of April 25, 2024 on the final rule could not be read (403). Several
search summaries (from queries 1 and 2 and a later search for the phrase) say its table gives the smaller-entity date as "three years after the
publication of the final rule or April 24, 2027". A Michigan municipal-website vendor, Shumaker
Technology Group, has that exact wording in a post of December 16, 2024, which also mentions an
"Advocacy" comment letter of October 17, 2023 without naming the SBA. The wording would come from
simple arithmetic: three years after Wednesday, April 24, 2024 is Saturday, April 24, 2027, while
the rule itself set Monday, April 26, 2027. This is a lead, not a finding: the SBA page itself was
not read.

## Limits

Six searches on one day. Four of them contain "April 24, 2027" or the April 24 pair, and the two
groups come from different queries, so the group sizes say little about how common each old date
was. Most candidates were dated after April 17, 2026 or carried no date, and pages edited in place
(WECO, AudioEye, ADA.gov's small entity guide) show new text under old dates, so they were not
counted. Pages were read through an AI web reader that quotes the page; the wrong old dates of CITI
Program, Spedtacular Daily Living and Accessible.org and Level Access's table were each read twice,
while the other exploratory pages (GovBuilt, City of Anywhere, Shumaker) were read once. Follow-up looked only for
pages a site search returns. The exploratory pages were not chosen by the plan and are not a sample.
